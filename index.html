About me

I build tools that solve real business problems — whether that means an AI-powered pipeline, a purpose-built application, or knowing when an existing product already does the job.

A well-designed product solves a real problem without adding new layers of friction. I believe the best tools feel invisible because they match the way people already think and work. Great design is not about decoration or trends. It is about understanding what someone needs to accomplish and removing every obstacle between them and that goal.

I come at this from the business side first. Fifteen years in financial markets and nine years running my own company in London taught me to think in terms of risk, cost, and return — not features. Every build starts with the same question: is the outcome worth more than what it costs to get there?

I don't have a CS degree and I no longer write code from memory. My focus is on the process that determines whether a solution actually delivers: defining what the real problem is before writing a line of code, choosing architecture that holds up under actual use, and cutting the scope that sounds good but delivers nothing. I use AI where it's strong and stay critical of it where it isn't — because the gap between a demo and a production tool is almost always in the decisions, not the syntax.

Restaurant Invoice Orchestration

Problem

A multi-outlet restaurant group buys everything through head office. Every supplier invoice has to be matched to the purchase order and the goods actually received, checked for duplicates and price errors, and approved by value. It is a slow manual chain where mistakes reach payment and approval rules are hard to audit.

Solution
  • Seven BPMN processes on Camunda 8: invoice-to-pay, 3-way match, supplier query, waste write-off, pour-cost reconciliation, stock reorder, and a shared approval routine
  • Deterministic where rules exist: a DMN decision table routes each approval to auto, manager, or director, with validation, duplicate detection, and invoice / PO / goods-received matching
  • Claude reviews each invoice's line items through Camunda's AI Agent connector and returns structured flags. Any flag or match discrepancy removes the auto-approve option
  • Approval limits live in admin-managed cluster variables, and who can approve what is enforced by Camunda authorizations, not the UI
Outcome
  • Working prototype tested live on Camunda 8.9: 8/8 approval-routing and 8/8 invoice-to-pay scenarios pass
  • Every model, deployment, test, and config change was made from Claude Code through the c8ctl CLI, not by clicking through the Console
  • AI flags issues, humans decide where money moves on uncertain ground

Bank Statement Pipeline

Problem

Preparing personal tax submissions means wrangling a year's worth of password-protected bank statements — different banks, different PDF formats — into categorised transactions. Tedious, manual, and easy to get wrong.

Solution
  • Batch unlock statements from any SA bank
  • Extract transactions via vision model
  • Reconcile every extraction against the statement's own printed totals — self-correcting on mismatch
  • Auto-categorise transactions (groceries, fuel, medical, etc.) using LLM classification
  • Dashboard with spend breakdowns, category totals, and exportable summaries
  • Fully client-side — no financial data leaves the browser
Outcome
  • Built to solve my own tax prep — hours of statement wrangling reduced to minutes
  • Dashboard gives instant visibility into spending patterns across all accounts
  • Privacy-first architecture makes it viable for a tax practitioner to trust with client data
